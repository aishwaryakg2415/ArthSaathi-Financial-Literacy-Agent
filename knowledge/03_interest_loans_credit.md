# Interest Rates, Loans and Credit Score

Source basis: RBI financial education material, RBI Fair Practices Code for lenders, credit bureau public FAQs.

## Simple interest
Interest = Principal × Rate × Time / 100
Example: ₹10,000 at 8% per year for 2 years → 10,000 × 8 × 2 / 100 = ₹1,600.

## Compound interest
Amount = P × (1 + r/n)^(n × t), where r is yearly rate (as decimal), n is times compounded per year, t is years.
Example: ₹10,000 at 8% compounded yearly for 2 years → 10,000 × 1.08² = ₹11,664 (interest ₹1,664).
Compounding helps savings grow faster, but also makes unpaid debt grow faster.

## Rule of 72
Years to double money ≈ 72 ÷ interest rate. At 8% → about 9 years.

## EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment)
EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n − 1)
P = loan amount, r = monthly interest rate (yearly rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), n = number of months.
Example: ₹1,00,000 at 12% per year for 12 months → r = 0.01 → EMI ≈ ₹8,885. Total paid ≈ ₹1,06,619.

## Flat rate vs reducing balance rate
- Flat rate: interest charged on the full original amount for the whole tenure. A "10% flat" loan really costs close to 18% on a reducing-balance basis.
- Reducing balance: interest only on what you still owe. Always compare loans using the reducing-balance rate / APR.

## APR (Annual Percentage Rate)
APR includes interest plus processing fees and other charges, so it shows the true yearly cost. RBI requires regulated lenders to disclose a Key Fact Statement (KFS) with APR before you sign. Always ask for the KFS.

## What is a "safe" interest rate for a loan? (general ranges, not advice)
Rates change with RBI's repo rate and your credit profile. Typical rough ranges in India:
- Home loan: lower range (around 8–10% per year).
- Car loan: around 9–12% per year.
- Education loan: around 8–13% per year; some government interest subsidy schemes exist.
- Gold loan: around 9–18% per year.
- Personal loan: around 10–24% per year.
- Credit card outstanding: very high, often 30–45% per year — avoid carrying a balance.
- Informal moneylenders / illegal apps: can exceed 60–100%+ per year — avoid.
A loan is "safer" when: the lender is RBI-regulated, the APR is clearly shown, the EMI is under about 30–40% of your monthly income (all EMIs combined), and there are no hidden charges.

## Credit score (CIBIL and other bureaus)
- A 3-digit number from 300 to 900 showing how reliably you repay. 750+ is generally considered good.
- Improves with: paying EMIs and card bills on time, keeping credit card usage below about 30% of the limit, having a mix of credit, avoiding many loan applications at once.
- You can check your report free once a year from each credit bureau.

## Savings interest (general)
- Savings account: low (around 2.5–4% per year at most large banks).
- Fixed Deposit (FD): higher, fixed for a chosen period; rates vary by bank and tenure.
- Bank deposits are insured by DICGC up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank (principal + interest).

## Red flags in a loan offer
- Lender asks for an upfront "processing fee" before approving.
- No Key Fact Statement or APR disclosed.
- App asks for access to your contacts and photos.
- Lender not listed as an RBI-registered bank or NBFC.
