# Budgeting and Personal Finance Basics

Source basis: RBI and NCFE (National Centre for Financial Education) financial literacy material, SEBI investor education.

## Why budget?
A budget tells your money where to go instead of wondering where it went. It helps you avoid debt, build savings and reach goals.

## The 50/30/20 rule (simple starting point)
- 50% Needs: rent, food, electricity, transport, school fees, EMIs.
- 30% Wants: eating out, shopping, entertainment, subscriptions.
- 20% Savings & debt repayment: emergency fund, investments, extra loan repayment.
Adjust for low incomes: even saving 5–10% regularly is a strong start.

Example for ₹20,000 monthly income: Needs ₹10,000 · Wants ₹6,000 · Savings ₹4,000.

## How to make a monthly budget (5 steps)
1. Write your total monthly income (after tax).
2. List fixed expenses (rent, EMI, fees) and variable expenses (food, travel, phone).
3. Track every expense for one month — use a notebook, your UPI app's history, or a spreadsheet.
4. Pay yourself first: move savings to a separate account on salary day.
5. Review at month-end and cut the biggest "wants".

## Emergency fund
Keep 3–6 months of essential expenses in a safe, easy-to-withdraw place (savings account, sweep-in FD, liquid fund). Build it before risky investments.

## Managing debt
- Pay high-interest debt first (credit card, personal loans).
- Never pay only the "minimum due" on a credit card — interest is charged on the rest.
- Keep total EMIs within about 30–40% of income.

## Saving and investing basics (education, not advice)
- Recurring Deposit (RD): save a fixed amount monthly at a bank.
- Fixed Deposit (FD): lump sum for a fixed period.
- Public Provident Fund (PPF): government-backed, 15-year, tax benefits.
- Mutual fund SIP: invest a fixed amount monthly; market-linked, so value can go up or down. Check the fund is from a SEBI-registered AMC.
- Match the product to the goal: short-term goals → low-risk options; long-term goals → can consider some market-linked options.
- Diversify; never put all savings in one place.
- Beware of anyone promising "guaranteed" high returns.

## Insurance first
- Term life insurance if others depend on your income.
- Health insurance for the family; government scheme Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY for eligible families.

## Good money habits
- Turn on transaction alerts.
- Check bank statements every month.
- Keep important documents (Aadhaar, PAN, bank passbook, policy papers) safe and add nominees to every account.
- Teach family members, especially elders, the basic scam safety rules.
