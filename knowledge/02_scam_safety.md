# Online Fraud & Scam Safety

Source basis: RBI "BE(A)WARE" booklet on financial frauds, Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C, cybercrime.gov.in), Department of Telecommunications Sanchar Saathi portal.

## Emergency: what to do if you are cheated
1. **Call 1930 immediately** (National Cyber Crime Helpline) or report at **cybercrime.gov.in**. Reporting within the first hours ("golden hour") gives the best chance of freezing the money.
2. Call your bank's official helpline and block your card / UPI / net banking.
3. Note the transaction ID, the fraudster's number/UPI ID, and take screenshots.
4. File a written complaint with your bank. Under RBI rules, if a fraud happens due to a third-party breach and you report it within **3 working days**, your liability is **zero**. Reporting within 4–7 working days gives limited liability.
5. Report suspicious calls/SMS/WhatsApp on the **Chakshu** facility of the Sanchar Saathi portal (sancharsaathi.gov.in).

## Common scams and the warning signs

### 1. "Enter PIN to receive money" / QR code scam
Fraudster says they are sending you money (refund, prize, buying your item on OLX) and sends a QR code or collect request. Scanning/approving with your PIN sends money **from** you.
Rule: PIN is never needed to receive money.

### 2. Fake KYC update / account block messages
SMS: "Your account will be blocked today, update KYC at this link." The link is a fake bank page that steals your login/OTP.
Rule: Banks do not ask for KYC through SMS links. Visit the branch or the official app.

### 3. OTP / CVV sharing calls
Caller pretending to be a bank, telecom, courier or government officer asks for the OTP "for verification".
Rule: Never share an OTP. An OTP is like the key to your money.

### 4. Screen-sharing app scam
"Customer care" asks you to install AnyDesk/TeamViewer/QuickSupport to "solve a problem" and then watches your screen and OTPs.

### 5. Fake customer care numbers
Numbers found on Google search or social media posing as customer care. Always use the number on the official website or the back of your card.

### 6. "Digital arrest" scam
Video callers pretending to be police, CBI, customs or courier say a parcel with drugs is in your name and you are "under digital arrest", then demand money.
Rule: No real agency arrests anyone over video call or asks for money transfers. Disconnect and call 1930.

### 7. Part-time job / task scams
"Earn ₹5,000 a day by liking videos / rating hotels." You get small payments first, then are asked to "invest" larger amounts that are never returned.

### 8. Investment / trading tips scams
WhatsApp/Telegram groups promising guaranteed high returns, fake trading apps showing fake profits. Check that any investment adviser is SEBI-registered.

### 9. Illegal loan apps
Instant loan apps not linked to an RBI-regulated bank/NBFC, with huge hidden charges and harassment of contacts. Check the lender's name on RBI's list of regulated entities before borrowing.

### 10. Lottery / KBC / prize scams
"You won ₹25 lakh, pay ₹10,000 processing fee." Real lotteries do not ask for a fee to release a prize.

### 11. Sextortion / fake video call
Unknown video call is recorded and used to blackmail. Do not pay; report at cybercrime.gov.in.

## Universal safety checklist
- Never share PIN, OTP, CVV, passwords or full card number.
- Check the sender's ID: official bank SMS come from registered sender IDs, not random 10-digit numbers.
- Do not click links in unexpected SMS, email or WhatsApp.
- Set transaction limits and turn on SMS/email alerts in your bank app.
- If it sounds too good to be true (guaranteed returns, prize you didn't enter), it is a scam.
- Take a pause: scammers create urgency. Hang up and call the official number yourself.
- Report fake deposit-taking schemes on RBI's Sachet portal (sachet.rbi.org.in).
